Square One

Three to ten flat pieces. One perfect square. Every set looks easy until you try to close the last gap.

Play Square One online

Free in your browser. No account needed.

How to play

Use every piece of one set to fill the square completely. You may turn a piece in 90° steps and flip it over. No gaps, no overlaps and no pieces left over.

The online version snaps pieces to half-square steps on a 6 × 6 grid, so every correct position is also a position the game recognises.

How the levels were made

Square One is not a cut-up square drawn by hand. A search program proposes thousands of ways to split a 6 × 6 square into pieces with at most five corners and edges along the grid or at 45°. Each candidate is then solved from scratch by four different solvers: two systematic computer strategies and two that imitate people. One always places the largest loose piece first. The other starts with the piece that fits most snugly against the frame and the pieces already placed, and it notices gaps no piece can fill.

A puzzle only counts as hard when both human-like strategies need a long search. People are not stuck if one natural approach works. Each strategy runs 31 times in a different order, and the score uses the median.

What makes it hard

Playtests with printed sets changed the score several times. People found the eight-piece puzzle the hardest, although the first score preferred nine and ten pieces. So the search learned to punish things that look complicated to a computer but are easy to guess:

  • Combs: narrow strips standing side by side along an edge, whose ends form one straight line.
  • Symmetry and repetition: a solution that looks the same when turned, like four identical bars around a square.
  • Few orientations: rectangles only lie two ways. Skewed pieces can lie eight ways, and you must try them in your head.
  • Two puzzles in one: a straight line that splits the square into two smaller tasks.

It also rewards pieces that do not touch the frame, because they defeat the habit of starting at the edge. All sets but one have exactly one solution, not counting turning the whole square. The nine-piece set has two.

Tips

  • Find the corners first: a right angle has only four places to go.
  • Pair the diagonal edges. Two 45° edges usually meet each other.
  • Count the area. When one gap is left, its size tells you which piece must go there.
  • Do not start with the biggest piece just because it is big. The search made sure that rarely works.

The printed puzzle

Square One is also designed as a printed puzzle: eight sets of 3 mm pieces in one 100 × 100 mm frame. The side of the frame says SneakyHard.com /square-one, the address of this page. Pieces may be turned over, so flip a piece if it seems not to fit.

Made by search, tested by people

Every SneakyHard level is chosen by a search program that models how people solve puzzles: few pieces, simple rules and as much challenge as they can hold. We build these solvers together with AI and refine them through playtests.
