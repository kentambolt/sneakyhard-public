How to play

Start on the marked tile and move up, down, left or right. Tiles disappear behind you, so you can never return. Visit every tile exactly once.

How the levels were made

Every board has exactly one solution. On the 5 × 5 grid the generator checked all 225 possible boards. Larger boards were found by simulated annealing: change a board a little, keep the change if it makes the board harder, and sometimes accept a worse one so the search does not get stuck.

Hardness is measured with a model of a sensible player. That player never makes a move that visibly ruins the board: cutting off a tile, or leaving several dead ends that would all need to be the last tile. With a lookahead of L moves, the player also spots moves that die within L moves. The score adds up how many bits of guessing it takes to solve the board on the first try for every lookahead from 0 to 8, so deep traps count many times.

What makes it hard

A well-known human trick for these puzzles is to visit edges and corners first. Boards where that trick fails get extra points. Boards that only differ by decoration, or whose solution looks too much like an already chosen board, are left out.

Tips

  • A tile with only two open neighbours tells you both of its moves.
  • Corners and dead ends usually must be the start or the end.
  • Before every move, check that you do not cut the remaining tiles in two.

Made by search, tested by people

Every SneakyHard level is chosen by a search program that models how people solve puzzles: few pieces, simple rules and as much challenge as they can hold. We build these solvers together with AI and refine them through playtests.
