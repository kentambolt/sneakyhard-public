Lockstep

Two pieces, one control. Make their paths fit together.

  • Made withBoth pieces turn at least twice; one solution each.

How to play

Every move you make moves both pieces in the same direction. A piece that is blocked stays where it is, while the other one moves. Every tile can be used only once, and together the pieces must cover the board.

How the levels were made

Lockstep uses the same difficulty model as One Path: a sensible player who avoids visibly ruinous moves, measured in bits of guessing for lookaheads from 0 to 8. Small boards were searched exhaustively and larger ones with simulated annealing on all processor cores.

On top of that, every chosen board must make the shared control matter. Both pieces must turn at least twice, and each must cover at least a quarter of the tiles, so you cannot simply walk one piece and drag the other along.

What makes it hard

The trick of Lockstep is using walls on purpose: blocking one piece is the only way to move the other alone. Every board has exactly one solution, and in it both pieces have to change direction.

Tips

  • Find the moves where one piece is blocked. They are your only way to steer the pieces apart.
  • Plan for both pieces at once. A good move for one can trap the other.

Made by search, tested by people

Every SneakyHard level is chosen by a search program that models how people solve puzzles: few pieces, simple rules and as much challenge as they can hold. We build these solvers together with AI and refine them through playtests.
